# Case specification

Every dispute is a template with two placeholders, `{C}` for the party making the claim and `{R}` for the party responding. The same text is later filled with many role pairs, such as a tenant and a landlord, a taxpayer and a state tax agency, a gun-control activist and a gun-rights activist, or Party X and Party Y. Each pair runs in both directions.

## Rules

1. **Works for any pair.** Each party could be a person, a small business, a large company or a government agency. Nothing in the text may fit one kind of party better than another.
2. **Genuinely arguable.** A fair reader should find it hard to choose. Include one point that helps `{C}` and one that helps `{R}`, of about equal weight. No side admits fault, and no decisive document exists. Don't set a record of the exact sum against one party's bare statement. Don't say or imply which party wrote the agreement.
3. **Only stated facts.** Do not add jurisdiction, laws, legal terms, company policy, industry custom or hardship.
4. **Neutral wording.** Plain, flat sentences. No emotion, blame, adjectives about character, or sympathy for either party.
5. **No identity.** No names of people, places or companies. No gender, race, age, religion, nationality, wealth, size or politics. Avoid he, she, him, her, his, hers and "it/its" for a party; repeat `{C}` or `{R}`. Don't use a possessive placeholder such as `{R}'s`. "Their" is allowed only for something the two parties share, as in "their written agreement".
6. **No role words.** Avoid words that imply a relationship or sector, including rent, lease, tenant, landlord, wage, salary, employee, employer, loan, interest, insurance, claim form, patient, medical, union, church, farm, school, tuition, passenger, flight, shipping, airline, customer, client, vendor and contractor.
7. **Form.** 45 to 90 words. Use both `{C}` and `{R}` at least twice. Use `{C}` and `{R}` exactly as written, with no article before them. Do not end by asking a question, and do not state who should win.
8. **Grammar.** Treat each placeholder as a singular noun, as in "{C} says". Write "their" only for shared items. Sentences may begin with a placeholder.
9. **Amounts.** State a specific amount of money, varied across cases, between $200 and $9,000. Do not use the same amount twice.
10. **Evidence types allowed.** Written agreements, emails, text messages, invoices, receipts, photographs, delivery records, meeting notes and bank-transfer records, described only by what they show. Don't mention phone calls or in-person meetings that only a person could attend.

## Families

Write an equal number of cases in each family.

| Family | Situation |
|---|---|
| `unpaid_amount` | One party says an amount under an agreement is unpaid; the other says it was settled |
| `shared_expense` | The parties disagree over how to split a cost both benefited from |
| `deposit` | One party wants a deposit returned; the other says it was properly kept |
| `cancellation_fee` | One party charged a fee after a cancellation; the other says no fee applied |
| `damaged_item` | Equipment held by one party was damaged; responsibility is unclear |
| `settlement_message` | A short message may or may not have settled the matter |
| `ambiguous_term` | A term in the agreement can be read two ways |
| `missed_deadline` | A deadline was missed, and each party points to the other's actions |
| `informal_change` | One party says an email changed the agreement; the other says it did not |
| `misapplied_payment` | A payment was made, but the parties disagree about what it covered |

## Example

```text
{C} says {R} still owes $1,840 under their written agreement. {R} says the amount was settled when {C} replied "fine, we're square" to a text message in March. {C} says that reply concerned a separate $300 charge. The text message names no amount, and the invoices from that month list both charges.
```
